Retail Customer Retention Analytics

A Power BI project about Target: which customers keep shopping, and which ones stop?

About this project

I used Power BI to study how Target's customers shop. The goal is to find loyal customers, repeat customers, and customers who stopped buying, so the store can keep more of them.

200 total customers
191 repeat customers
49 churned customers
24.5% churn rate

Words used in this project

Churn
A customer who stopped buying from the store.
Repeat customer
A customer who comes back and buys again.
Loyalty tier
A reward level in the loyalty program, for example Elite or Plus.
CLV (Customer Lifetime Value)
How much a customer is worth to the store over time.
DAX
The formula language used in Power BI to do calculations.

The 4 dashboards

1. Customer Retention Overview

  • The overall churn rate is 24.50%.
  • Most customers are repeat customers.
  • Medium income customers churn the most. Low income customers churn the least.

2. Loyalty Program and Promotions

  • 39% of transactions used a promotion.
  • The Elite tier has the highest churn. The Plus tier has the lowest.
  • Customers earn more loyalty points than they use.
  • Apparel has the most transactions.

3. Store and Channel Performance

  • Superstores have the highest average purchase (423).
  • Online has the lowest average purchase (397).
  • Churn is 24.50% across all store types.

4. Customer Segments and Value

  • The Central region has the highest customer lifetime value.
  • Customers aged 51+ buy most often.
  • The Elite tier has the highest customer lifetime value.
  • Most customers are in the Mid-Tier purchase group.

How I built it

  1. Loaded 5 datasets into Power BI: customers, transactions, stores, loyalty program, and churn.
  2. Cleaned the data in Power Query and added columns for membership length, transaction year, and transaction month.
  3. Connected the tables using Customer_ID and Store_ID.
  4. Wrote DAX measures for churn rate, average purchase, CLV, and more.
  5. Built 4 dashboards with charts and filters.

What Target can do next

  • Reach out to customers who stopped buying and give them a reason to return.
  • Make loyalty rewards more attractive so customers use their points.
  • Check stores and regions with low activity to find out why.
  • Plan promotions around what customers like to buy.
  • Update the dashboard regularly to follow trends.
